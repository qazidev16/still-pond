    Why each part works

    • Stream: a visuospatial puzzle. Overthinking runs on the same mental "screen" as images and spatial tasks. Turning shapes takes up that screen. Studies with Tetris, and with other shape-turning tasks, found fewer intrusive images. Study
    • Breathe: cyclic sighing. Two breaths in, one long breath out. In a Stanford trial, 5 minutes a day of this improved mood more than mindfulness meditation did. The ring fills over those 5 minutes. Study
    • Let go: cognitive defusion. Each thought you write gets reframed as "I'm having the thought that…". That reframe makes a thought feel less believable and less upsetting. Study
    • Park for later: worry postponement. You move a worry to a set worry time instead of fighting it now. A meta-analysis of 7 trials found a small reduction in daily worry. Meta-analysis
    • Check-in. Rating your mind before and after lets you see what actually helps you, so you know what to reach for next time.

    This is a calming tool, not treatment. If overthinking keeps getting in the way of sleep or work, talk to a professional.
